Proposition · système de blocs

17 composants pour tout le site, au lieu de 73 blocs

Le site a été construit page par page. Chaque page a fini par inventer sa version des mêmes blocs. Voici le système que je recommande : une seule façon de faire chaque chose, des variantes réglées par propriétés, et des blocs uniques seulement quand on n’a pas le choix.

1

blocs vraiment uniques, parce qu’interactifs ou animés et propres à une page

Ce que je constate

  • Les mêmes blocs, refaits à chaque page. Un repère, un titre, un texte : why-card, methodology-card, difference-card, glance-stat, rp-card, rp-step, rp-stat, cartes du case study. Huit classes pour une seule idée.
  • Mes protos en ont ajouté. Les classes rp-* ont servi à valider le rendu. Elles ne doivent pas partir telles quelles dans Webflow : elles se fondent dans le système.
  • Pas d’échelle d’espacement. Les gaps changent d’un bloc à l’autre (audit dans Petits éléments, rubrique Espacements).
  • Trois fonds clairs pour le même rôle (blanc, lightpurple, gris) et deux tailles de titre de section qui se concurrencent.

Ce que je conseille

  • Une page = une suite de Sections. Une Section = un fond, un en-tête, un bloc de contenu. Dans Webflow : un composant Section avec un emplacement (slot), un composant En-tête, et 12 blocs qu’on glisse dans l’emplacement.
  • Fixer les fondations d’abord : fonds, espacements, rayons, boutons, titres. C’est ce qui rend les blocs cohérents entre eux, plus que les blocs eux-mêmes.
  • Construire dans cet ordre : Section et En-tête, Cartes, Chiffres, Média + texte, Cas. Puis migrer page par page, en commençant par les pages produit, déjà refaites en proto.
  • Nettoyer les classes en dernier, une fois les pages migrées, pour ne rien casser en route.

How we approach it

Evidence to probability to value

In either mode, Portfolio Optimizer follows the same transparent logic: assemble the right evidence, quantify what it supports, and translate it into action.

Apply the right mix of causal modeling, TTE, RCT and RWD integration, or mechanistic simulation.

Quantify the conclusion and its uncertainty

Treat historical rates as a prior that widens when a program is novel or comparability is weak.

Calibrate predictive, causal, and mechanistic evidence by context, performance, bias, and comparability.

Strengthen the evidence behind your next decision

Two complementary ways
to understand treatment effects

Patients differ in their expected outcomes and in how much they benefit from treatment. Response Optimizer addresses both: prognostic modeling accounts for baseline differences to improve precision, while HTE analysis examines differences in treatment benefit that the overall average may obscure.

Can baseline information improve precision?

Differences in baseline characteristics can explain part of the variation in trial outcomes. Prognostic modeling captures this predictable component to support more precise treatment-effect estimation and evaluate potential gains in statistical power.
Problem
Predictable variation remains mixed into the endpoint.
PhaseV approach
Validate and lock one prognostic score for prespecified covariate adjustment.
Development value
Sharpen the estimate without changing the estimand or trial operations.

Who benefits more because of treatment?

The average can hide a treatment that works for the right patients. Meaningful benefit in one population can be diluted by little or no benefit elsewhere, leaving the all-comer result modest, inconclusive, or negative.
Problem
Overall results may obscure differences in treatment benefit.
PhaseV approach
Estimate conditional treatment effects, assess robustness across methods, and evaluate clinically interpretable subgroup candidates.
Development value
Assess whether the evidence supports a population-focused development strategy and what requires further confirmation.

FAQs

Questions, answered

What is Trial Optimizer?

Trial Optimizer is a simulation workbench for clinical trial design. It lets you set up candidate designs, vary the assumptions that drive them, simulate performance across scenarios, and refine the design you select.

Which designs can I compare?

Fixed, adaptive, and Bayesian designs, along with your own customized designs. You can compare them side by side across the same set of scenarios.

How does it handle uncertainty in my assumptions?

You vary treatment and control effects, recruitment, allocation, interim strategy, and other assumptions, then simulate each design across those scenarios. The results show how each design performs when assumptions miss, so you can see which designs hold up.

What does the GenAI Assistant do?

It retrieves relevant public studies to inform placebo or standard-of-care response. You review each source and select the reference trials you want to use.

How does the optimization work?

After you select a design, PhaseV’s proprietary stochastic optimization algorithm searches the parameter space for further improvement against your objectives and constraints.

What can I export?

The selected design, with supporting evidence, an audit trail, and reproducibility settings.

Does Trial Optimizer forecast enrollment?

You can vary recruitment assumptions and see their effect on design performance. For forecasts built from site-level and regional data, use ClinOps Optimizer.

Do I need the rest of the PhaseV platform?

No. Trial Optimizer is complete on its own. When connected to the broader platform, reviewed insights can enter the scenario space and approved parameters can return to connected documents for expert review.

How long does setup take?

You can set up a design in minutes, starting from a prior design or a guided setup.

Causal models can extend what we learn from trials

Oncology Phase 2 studies are often small, split across biomarker-defined subgroups, and frequently single-arm, leaving a fragmented view of disease biology and treatment response. To make sense of that fragmentation, we need a way to connect patient measurements, biological mechanisms, treatment, and outcomes. Causal models provide a framework for connecting data to biological mechanisms and exploring why patients respond differently, how outcomes vary across cohorts, and what might happen under alternative biological or treatment conditions. That missing layer can fundamentally change how the results are interpreted.
