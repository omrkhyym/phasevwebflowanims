Why ClinOps Optimizer?

Choose sites with confidence

Experience is one input. ClinOps Optimizer is not tied to existing site relationships. It draws on data from many sources to find the sites that can reach the right patients for your protocol, and you decide what to prioritize for each study.
01

Start with your protocol

We match each site’s patients to your eligibility criteria and expected screen failure.
02

Account for the landscape

Competing trials and standard of care affect where patients are available. We factor both in when choosing countries and sites.
03

Combine your data with new data

Add your own trial data and your CRO’s historical site data to current trial, real-world, geographic, and competitive data. The result is a clearer view of the best mix of countries and sites for each study, and a trial that runs faster with fewer resources.

Find new sites

Keep proven sites and see emerging centers with strong potential for your study.

Set your own priorities

Favor experienced sites on one study, newer sites on another, or sites with fewer competing trials on a third. See why each site ranks where it does, adjust the weights, add what you know, and compare options.

How the forecast works

Forecasts you can edit

Every forecast shows its inputs, assumptions, and uncertainty. Change any of them and the forecast reruns.

Data

Public trial data, licensed real-world data, site-level operational data, and sponsor trial data, combined for your protocol.

Modeling

Bayesian hierarchical models learn how sites and countries recruit. Monte Carlo simulation shows the range of likely enrollment and dates.

Transparency

See what drives each recommendation and how confident the model is. AI helps read the protocol, find comparable studies, and set up scenarios. The forecast itself comes from statistical models.

FAQs

Questions, answered

How do I choose the right clinical trial sites?

Match each site’s patients to your protocol’s eligibility criteria, then rank sites on protocol fit, recruitment potential, and operational performance. ClinOps Optimizer does this and shows the reasons behind each ranking.

How can I reduce enrollment delays?

Test your eligibility criteria early, choose sites that can reach the right patients, and track enrollment against a forecast. ClinOps Optimizer supports all three.

How is ClinOps Optimizer different from my CRO’s recommendations?

It works alongside your CRO. PhaseV draws on data from many CROs and studies, so it can surface sites your CRO has not worked with, and it explains why sites succeed.

How does it handle new sites or sites with little data?

It borrows from related data, such as a high-performing investigator’s past enrollment at a previous institution, and shows the added uncertainty in the forecast.

Where does the data come from, and is it protected?

Public sources, licensed real-world data, partnerships, and sponsor data shared under your confidentiality terms. PhaseV works only with de-identified or anonymized data.

What do I need to start, and how long does it take?

A protocol, an indication, and target geographies are enough. Historical trial data and site lists make the results more specific. If your indication is already live on our self-serve platform, you can have results in 48 hours. Otherwise, expect 2-3 weeks.
