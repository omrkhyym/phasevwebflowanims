The challenge

More data does not automatically create more conviction

Asset decisions rest on evidence that is scattered, partial, or conflicting. Combining it without double counting or hiding disagreement is hard, and a wrong call can mean a misdirected program or an overpaid deal.

Historical averages are too broad

They offer context but miss what makes your asset different: its mechanism, endpoint, patients, and setting.

Models see different parts of the problem

Predictive models find patterns. Causal and mechanistic models test what drives outcomes. No single lens is enough.

A point estimate can hide the real decision risk

Teams need to know what drives the estimate, how uncertain it is, and which upcoming evidence could change it.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What decisions does Portfolio Optimizer support?

Two kinds. It helps you prioritize the path, meaning which indication, combination, population, or sequence should come next. It also helps you calibrate the bet, meaning the probability of success for a specific late-stage asset, with the evidence behind it. Both are used for portfolio strategy, lifecycle planning, and business development diligence.

How is this different from the benchmarks and consultants we already use?

Benchmarks give you industry averages. Consultants give you expert judgment. Portfolio Optimizer adds a quantitative, asset-specific view that combines both with biological and real-world evidence, and it shows its reasoning. You can see what drives each number and test the assumptions yourself.

Why causal modeling instead of standard predictive AI?

Predictive models find patterns, but they can’t tell you why an outcome happens or who truly benefits from a drug. Causal models are built to answer “why” and “what if.” They are also transparent, so your team can inspect and challenge them instead of trusting a black box.

How do we know the models are reliable?

We test them against real, published trials. In oncology, our PD-L1 blocker model closely reproduced the patient-level variability seen in the published atezolizumab trial. Every estimate also shows its evidence, model agreement, and uncertainty. [add quantitative validation metric if available]

What if the asset is novel or evidence is limited?

We rely more on causal and mechanistic modeling and on related indications, and we treat historical rates as a starting point, not an answer. When evidence is thin or conflicting, the probability range widens, so uncertainty stays visible.
